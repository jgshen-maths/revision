Mission C1

Fractions as decimals

Type 1 ÷ 3 into a calculator.
0.3333333333

Does it ever stop? And why does $\dfrac18$ stop at $0.125$? Let's find out.

Let's do it together
Detective game: stop or repeat?

Tap each fraction. It goes green if its decimal stops, red if it repeats.

Tap a fraction to see its remainders.
Spot the pattern 🤔

Break the bottom numbers into prime bricks:

Stops: $4=2\times2$
$8=2\times2\times2$
$20=2\times2\times5$

Only 2s and 5s: the same bricks as $10=2\times5$!

Repeats: $6=2\times3$
$15=3\times5$
$7$ and $11$
A 3, 7 or 11 is hiding inside. That is why they never stop.

Shortcut card

$\dfrac38=3\div8$: write $3.000$ and divide, carrying remainders.

Bottom number only 2s and 5s → stops. Anything else inside → repeats: $\dfrac23=0.\dot6$, $\dfrac17=0.\dot14285\dot7$.

Mission C2

Ordering fractions

Amina scored $\dfrac{18}{25}$. Ben scored $\dfrac{37}{50}$.

Who did better? The pieces are different sizes, so we can't just compare the top numbers. We need same-size pieces.

Let's do it together
Play: the fraction-bar comparer
Shortcut card

Same top number? The smaller bottom number is bigger: $\dfrac7{11}>\dfrac7{13}$.

Different bottoms? Make them the same: $\dfrac34=\dfrac9{12}$, $\dfrac23=\dfrac8{12}$. Percentages? Make the bottom 100.

Negatives: closer to zero is bigger.

Mission C3

Subtracting mixed numbers

Bim ran $8\dfrac58$ km. Yolander ran $10\dfrac34$ km.

How much further did Yolander run? Mixed numbers have two parts: wholes and pieces. We deal with each part on its own.

Let's do it together
Shortcut card

1. Make the pieces the same size. 2. Wholes take away wholes. 3. Pieces take away pieces.

Not enough pieces? Break a whole: $4\dfrac24=3\dfrac64$. Then check by adding back.

Mission C5

Dividing by a fraction

3 pizzas. Everyone eats half a pizza.

How many people can eat? That is $3\div\dfrac12$. Dividing can make a number bigger!

Let's do it together
Play: how many pieces fit?
Shortcut card

$4\div\dfrac23$: “how many $\dfrac23$ fit into 4?”

Flip and multiply: $4\div\dfrac23=4\times\dfrac32=6$.
